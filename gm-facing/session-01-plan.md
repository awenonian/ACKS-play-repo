# GM-Facing: Session 1 — "Grey Cloth"

**PC:** Teodor Vask, Venturer 1, **2 hp**. Solo.
**Location:** Tellys Ford, the Borderlands. **Season:** late spring.

---

## The Design Problem, Stated Plainly

A solo 2 hp character dies to one arrow. ACKS assumes a party. Therefore **session 1's
first act is not a dungeon — it is hiring people.** The class supports 5 henchmen at +1
loyalty. The session must make crew-building feel like the obvious, attractive move rather
than a chore, and must not punish the player for the two rounds it takes to realize it.

Corollary: the Cistern must be **negotiable**. There is a talking solution, a burning
solution, and a fighting solution, and the fighting solution is the worst one. That is the
correct shape for this character.

---

## The Opening Position (all true, all known to Teodor)

Last autumn the quartermaster at **Kalyx Gate** placed a standing order through the Tellys
Ford factors: grey wool broadcloth, bulk, delivered before midsummer. This is the safest
order in the world. Grey is the colour of death — worn only in mourning — and forts bury
men. Teodor borrowed **120 gp from Vitte Sarn** against it and bought thirty bolts.

In early spring the order was cancelled. The note gave a reason of three words:
**"No further need."**

Nobody thought about it twice. Everyone assumed it meant the fort was economizing, or that
the quartermaster had found a cheaper supplier in Krysea. Teodor is holding thirty bolts of
a fabric that has exactly one use, in a province of some four thousand people, and the debt
falls due on **market-day, twelve days out.**

*(This is the campaign's central clue and the player is holding it in his hands as a
business problem. He is not meant to solve it now. He is meant to remember it later.)*

---

## The Job

**Lady Dessa Korvane** holds the toll rights on the Tellys Ford crossing. For five weeks
something has been squeezing traffic on the **Cistern road** — the old cut that runs south
past a ruined Krysean-War fort. Two mule-trains turned back. A shepherd boy went missing.
Traffic that doesn't cross the ford doesn't pay her toll, so this is costing her money,
which is why she will actually pay to fix it.

**Her opening offer: 60 gp** to make the road usable again. She expects to be haggled and
respects it. She will go to **90** on a good reaction roll, **110** if Teodor argues the
toll arithmetic at her (i.e. if the player makes the *economic* case rather than the moral
one — reward that with +1). Anything she pays, she pays on completion.

She will also, if asked, **advance the loan of a cart and mule** — she'd rather he owe her
than owe Sarn.

**Whatever's in the ruin, he keeps.** She says so unprompted; she is not interested in
century-old Krysean coin, she is interested in tolls. *(Under imperial treasure law she'd
be entitled to half as landowner — she is waiving it, and it's worth having her say so out
loud, because it establishes her as someone who gives away small things to get big ones.)*

## Hireable in Tellys Ford

Mercenary wage rates and henchman rules: see `equipment/hirelings-and-henchmen.md` before
finalizing prices in play. Rough shape:

- **Bela Ruk** — 0th level, Krysean, ex-militia, spear and shield, wants 6 gp/month or a
  share. Steady, unimaginative, will not go underground for less than a share. *Speaks
  Krysean; warms +1 if Teodor uses it.*
- **The Ferrin brothers, Oss and Little Oss** — 0th level, cheap, load-bearers with
  hatchets. Will absolutely run. Morale is the problem, not courage.
- **Nikkos Varr** — 0th level, a torchbearer and lamp-boy, 15, wants to see a ruin.
  *The obvious henchman-to-PC succession pick if Teodor dies. Keep him alive and
  interesting.*
- **Sister Yolen** — lay sister of Calefa at the burning-ground; not for hire, but will
  give away rough first aid and opinions.

**If Teodor tries to go in alone, let him, and let the goblin sentry's first sling stone
be rolled honestly.** Don't fudge it. But do give him the sentry's *reaction roll* first —
see below — because the goblins are not hostile by default and the encounter is winnable
with words.

## THE CISTERN

A Krysean-War border fort, abandoned ~100 years, three miles south of the ford. Above
ground: a rubble mound, one standing gate-arch, a curtain wall reduced to a ridge in the
grass. Locals call it the Cistern because the only intact part is the waterworks below.

**Light: none below ground.** Teodor has a lantern and a tinderbox and no torches.
*(This matters. Note it and don't remind him.)*

### Who's in it

**Nine goblins and two young** — refugees, not raiders. They came east out of the Waste
six weeks ago and they are not going back. They are hungry, cold, badly armed, and
frightened, and they have been robbing the road because the road is what there is.

Goblin stats, derived from `armies/troop-characteristics.md` (Goblin Light Infantry /
Slingers — same rulebook, verified):

> **AC 3 · Move 60′ · HD 1−1 · 4 hp · 1 attack · damage by weapon · Save NM · Morale −1**
> XP 5 each (HD "less than 1" = 5 base, no special abilities).
> Armament here is worse than the army list assumes: leather scraps, **AC 2**, slings
> (1d4) and short spears (1d6). Two have shields (AC 3).

**Morale −1 is the load-bearing number.** These goblins break. Engineer situations where
morale gets checked and the fight ends without dice-on-dice attrition.

**Grett** — the one in charge, not a chief so much as the one who kept count. HD 1, 6 hp,
AC 3, short sword. Speaks Goblin only.

### The language ruling (make this call once, out loud, and stick to it)

Teodor speaks **Ancient Zaharan** (the parent tongue) and **Orc** (a daughter tongue).
Goblin is Orc's sibling — both descend from Ancient Zaharan per `setting/entries-1.md`.

**Ruling: Teodor can make himself understood in Goblin, haltingly.** Treat it as functional
for negotiation but not for nuance — he can trade, threaten, and ask simple questions; he
cannot be subtle, and he will be understood as speaking oddly and archaically, like a man
using scripture to order dinner. This should be *charming and unnerving* to the goblins in
about equal measure. **Give him +1 on the reaction roll for it** — nobody has ever tried
talking to them before.

### Map

1. **The Gate Stair** — stepped shaft down into the head tank. One goblin sentry, slinger.
   *Roll reaction, not initiative, unless Teodor opens with violence.*
2. **The Head Tank** — vaulted, 40′ × 60′, dry, floor under three feet of silt. The camp.
   Fire, bones, stolen mule-tack, a child's shoe. **The shepherd boy is dead** — of cold and
   a broken leg, not eaten; they took his boots and left him. His body is in the silt at the
   north end. This is a real and ugly fact and should not be softened, and it is also not a
   murder.
3. **The Settling Channels** — waist-deep water, four narrow vaulted runs connecting the
   tanks. Slippery: any fighting here risks going down (**prone: −4 to attack, attackers
   get +2**). Teodor at 2 hp should be *terrified* of this room and correctly so.
4. **The Overflow Sump** — flooded, roughly 8′ deep. **The paychest is here**: an
   iron-strapped box holding **620 gold suns**, a century of Krysean-War garrison pay that
   nobody ever came back for. Retrieving it requires rope, light, and someone willing to go
   under — which is a *henchman problem*, deliberately.
5. **The Milestone** — the fort's builders reused older stone. Set upside-down in the
   cistern's south wall is a **boundary marker in Ancient Zaharan**, listing road-distances.
   Teodor can read it. It gives three destinations. Two are meaningless (dead towns).
   The third reads: **"SARRU-KALYX — XI"**.

   Eleven of something, to a place called Sarru-Kalyx. There is a fort eleven miles south of
   here called Kalyx Gate. **This is the campaign key and it is being handed over in session
   one as a curiosity.** Do not underline it. Let him find it, read it, and decide for
   himself whether it's worth anything. If he asks, an honest answer is: old places keep
   their names, and Auran forts are usually named for the hill they sit on.

### What the goblins know and will trade for food or safe passage

- They came from the Waste, past the south fort, six weeks ago.
- They will not go back that way. The phrase, translated as literally as Teodor's halting
  Goblin allows: **"the hungry hill."**
- They mean Kalyx Gate. They will not elaborate because they do not have words for it;
  what they have is the certainty of an animal about a place that smells wrong.
- If pressed: no beastman clan of the Waste will come within sight of that hill. This is
  the *reason* the roads near Kalyx Gate are the safest in the Borderlands — a fact the
  Aurans credit entirely to Prefect Ostren's vigilance.

### Also present

- **Rats in the silt.** Not a fight. Atmosphere and a reason to fear a cut at 2 hp.
- **The road itself.** Once the goblins are dealt with — killed, driven off, hired,
  fed, or *relocated* — the job is done and Korvane pays.

## The Solution Space (rank-ordered by how well it serves this character)

1. **Feed them and move them on.** They're starving. Food is cheap. Korvane wants the road
   clear, not corpses; she will pay for either. Teodor could buy grain for a few gp and
   broker a passage. This is the best outcome and the class is built for it.
2. **Hire them.** Nine bodies who owe him. Wildly ambitious, complicates everything
   downstream, and is *legal* only in the loosest sense. Allow it. It's great.
3. **Drive them off** with fire, oil, noise, and a mercenary or two. Works fine.
4. **Kill them all.** Achievable with four hired spears. Costs money, costs goodwill with
   Sister Yolen, and forecloses the "hungry hill" clue unless he takes a prisoner.

**The grey cloth solution is not here.** Do not let a clever player solve the debt inside
the Cistern except by the paychest. The cloth is a slow problem.

## XP Budget

- 9 goblins @ 5 = 45; Grett (HD 1) = 10. **55 XP** if all defeated (defeated includes
  outsmarted or driven off, per `adventures/experience-from-adventures.md`).
- Paychest: **620 XP**.
- Korvane's fee: reward treasure, **XP when received**.
- Divided by shares — henchmen take **half shares**. Then **+5%** for Teodor.

A clean session lands Teodor somewhere around 400–550 XP. Level 2 at 1,500. Correct pace.

---

## Things To Not Do

- Don't have anyone in Tellys Ford find the cancelled cloth order suspicious. Nobody has
  connected anything. The whole point is that it looks like ordinary bad luck.
- Don't let an NPC deliver the Kalyx Gate situation as a speech. Kalyx Gate is **good news**
  in this province and everyone says so warmly and briefly.
- Don't put a report of open threads at the end of a turn. Keep it in `open-threads.md`
  and surface it only in a lull.
